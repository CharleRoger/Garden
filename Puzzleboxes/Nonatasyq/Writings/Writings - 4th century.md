A treatise by an unknown author:
> Some of the most fruitful are the parable chants and plays. One of these is [[Prose translations of chants#I am just a hunter (Chant 135)|the chant of the hunter]], whose author composed it wisely to guide people in their paths. Each parable has four parts: the introduction, the fixation, the withering, and the release. In the first part, the chant reveals the central character living his normal life, with the character having his own personality and various desires. The listeners understand that he is like them and when hearing the character, the listeners hear themselves. The second part is the fixation, which begins when the character encounters something in line with a desire and becomes fixated on that thing. The audience understands that this fixation is like their own and attaches their own fixation to that of the character. The second part ends when the character is confronted with either abandoning the fixation in favor of something else or continuing with it. The character chooses the latter, which leads to the third part, where the character starts to neglect everything else in favor of the fixation. The character loses their personality and other desires and may also neglect their family or health. In the listener's mind, the character becomes identical to the fixation of the character, which is also of the listener. During the withering, the character degrades further and further, until this process is eventually stopped by the only thing that can stop it, the death of the character. This brings the fourth part of the chant, the release. The audience's mind is shocked, as the character is ripped away from them. Those with strong emotions often cry, yet not only of sadness but also of relief, since the death frees the audience from the suffering and the fixation of the character: as the listener has made the character the same as his [the listener's] own fixation, the death of the character also brings the death of the fixation. So, understand that anyone who claims these plays and chants are mere entertainment is without understanding. They are some of the most important, showing the futility of fixations and helping to clear the audience of them.
